# Court — proof audit before a 50,000-sheet run

Four architectures, not four adjustments. Each replaced the previous one
because a named defect could not be reached by tuning.

| Pass | What was replaced | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | frame → **tonal mass** | no silhouette at 3m; every part of the sheet one tone |
| 2 | outer-edge medallions → **band-midline** | 13mm of the head device sat past the trim, struck through the knife on every copy |
| 3 | pale emboss ring → **struck seal** on a turned field | the seal read as a hole, cold against a warm sheet |
| 4 | six discs → **four silhouettes**; uniform tile → **runs with joints**; centred → **off-axis apparatus** | it read as generated, and the mechanism was repetition plus a universal centre line |

## Three-Distance Test — current verdict

**3 metres — passes.** Deep bordered mass against a luminous reserved panel,
with metal at the head, the four corners and the three mid-sides. The
silhouette is unmistakable and it is tonal, not ornamental.

**1 metre — passes.** The border has rhythm: four strapwork runs with
expansion joints, a shaped head cartouche, corner fans, mid-side lozenges,
and a plain pause either side of every interruption. The medallion family has
four distinct silhouettes — cartouche, fan, lozenge, disc — and the disc is
spent **once**, on the seal, which is why the seal reads as *the* seal. The
ceremonial column is on the axis; the apparatus deliberately is not.

**20 centimetres — passes.** Guilloché in the seal field and in the minor
register, fine-text rails carrying the live serial, corner arcs at five radii,
an anti-copy ruling at 8°, and a UV layer. There is more of one geometry the
closer it is looked at.

## What still stops this being approved artwork

Named rather than hidden, because the brief asked for the list:

1. **The strapwork repeats within each run.** The runs are separated by joints,
   but *inside* a run it is one tile at one scale. A royal border varies scale
   along its length; this one does not yet.
2. **The head cartouche sits proud of the panel's top rule** and crosses the
   fine-text rail. At 20cm that reads as a collision, not a layer.
3. **The right vertical band is cut asymmetrically** by the panel's inner rule,
   so the left and right runs are not the same length.
4. **The seal carries a cold grey emboss halo** against warm gold. The relief
   simulation is right in principle and slightly wrong in value.
5. **Only Court has had four passes.** Chancery, Intaglio, Letterpress and
   Meridian have had one each.
6. **Nothing has been printed.** Foil adhesion, emboss depth, hairline survival
   and the fluorescent response are stated from known press behaviour, not
   measured on these plates.

Items 1–4 are reachable in a fifth pass. Item 6 is not reachable here at all.
