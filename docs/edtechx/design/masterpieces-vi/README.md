# Six plates — a permanent visual identity

Six architectures, each composed **onto press plates** from the first stroke,
each derived from a compositional tradition rather than from the engine's own
habits.

| | Authority sits in | Enclosure | Spends on | Plates | ≈ cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Collegiate** | the seal | none | emboss die | 4 | 5.6× |
| **Chancery** | the arms | one rule | foil | 7 | 10.7× |
| **Court** | the frame | illuminated | foil ×2 | 8 | 15.1× |
| **Intaglio** | the guilloché | engine field | guilloché | 7 | 8.5× |
| **Letterpress** | the title | one rule | deboss die | 5 | 10.0× |
| **Meridian** | the name | none | one foil mark | 5 | 6.5× |

Two of the six have **no border at all**. Two have **no foil at all**. One puts
the guilloché in front rather than behind. One is off-centre.

## Composite is not separation

The first render of these plates painted the emboss, deboss and varnish plates
as solid black in the composite, putting black rectangles across two of the six.
A composite simulates a printed sheet; a separation instructs a press. Relief has
no colour — a lit wall and a shadow wall. Coating is felt, not seen. UV is
invisible in daylight and draws nothing.

## Not yet done

Three of the six are under their own ink-coverage target — Chancery (0.04 vs
0.14), Court (0.11 vs 0.30), Intaglio (0.04 vs 0.42). The measurement is
reported rather than the target quietly lowered. Those three need another pass:
Chancery's arms are not yet a third of the sheet, Court's field is a void, and
Intaglio's lathe work is too faint to be the identity it claims to be.

**Nothing has been printed.** Every plate is a specification.
