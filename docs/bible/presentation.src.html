What changed in Edition II

Edition I described a product. Edition II describes a group.

Edition I was an AI operating system for knowledge work, with Arabic and Islamic scholarship as first-class domains. That description was correct and remains true — it is now Volume III, Division 1: one product line among many.

The reframing is structural, not cosmetic. Edition I asked what should our product do? Edition II asks what does an institution need in order to be modern, and which of those needs can StromeX meet, at what price, with what margin, at what scale, in which country, by which year?

The roadmap

A phase ends when its gate is passed, not when its years elapse.

2027 – 2030

Foundation

Build quietly. Perfect the products. Build trust instead of chasing growth. Optimised for quality, satisfaction, reputation and recurring mix — explicitly not for rapid profitability.

Will this decision make StromeX stronger in 2031?

2031 – 2035

Race

Expand aggressively, having earned the right to. Enterprise and government motions open, international offices, the major product launches, the first acquisitions.

How do we scale without compromising quality, trust or customer satisfaction?

2036 – 2040

Global Scale

Become a globally recognised technology company — and, more importantly, one that no longer depends on the people who started it.

How do we become durable enough to outlive our founders?

The financial position

Three scenarios, not one target.

The stated ambition is a group valued at $15–40 billion within ten to twenty years. The corpus takes that seriously by modelling what would have to be true — and by saying plainly which scenario the company should plan against.

The Entrenched Provisions

Amendable only by board supermajority, published rationale, and thirty days’ notice

  1. Verification is free for the verifier, forever. A graduate’s credential is never held hostage to their institution’s subscription.
  2. Customer data belongs to the customer. Never sold, never brokered, never used to train third-party models, always exportable free and complete.
  3. Children’s data receives the strictest handling in the group. Never monetised, never profiled, never advertised against.
  4. No bribery, ever, in any market, for any contract. Losing business for this reason is a correct outcome.
  5. We do not build surveillance, weapons, or systems designed to persecute.
  6. High-stakes decisions about a person are made by a person — named, recorded and accountable.
  7. We disclose our failures before we are asked, with a timeline and a fix.
  8. The free tier is real, and its boundary is stated plainly rather than discovered at the moment of frustration.
  9. We do not sell what we cannot deliver.
  10. A price is honoured for its term, and increases are announced, capped and explained.

The logo decision

Four candidates. One survives reduction.

The criteria, in priority order: does it survive at 16 px in a browser tab, embossed on a PVC card, and etched on a turnstile? Does it hold in one colour? Can a Lagos printer reproduce it correctly on the first attempt without a colour proof?

A — Chrome double-chevron

Confident and legible, but the chevron pair is heavily occupied territory in tech identity. Ownability is the weakness. Archived.

B — Ribbon infinity-S

The most beautiful as an image and the weakest as a logo. Multi-stop gradients and self-intersecting ribbons collapse below 64 px and cannot be embossed. An illustration, not an identity. Archived.

C — Gold hexagonal S

The most complete submission — it already thinks in app icon, monochrome, embossed and neon. But gold-on-black reads luxury, not infrastructure. Its system sheet is adopted as the brand-kit template; the mark is retained for the Elite tier and premium imprints.

D — Interlocking S · Selected

The only candidate constructed geometrically rather than pictorially. One interlocking form, one accent element. It reduces to a favicon, survives single-colour reproduction, embosses on a card, engraves on metal, and prints correctly without a proof cycle. It reads as infrastructure — the category we are actually in — and it will still look correct stamped on a government contract, a smart gate, a textbook spine and a browser tab on the same day.

The final instruction

If StromeX ever has to choose between being large and being trusted, it chooses trusted.

That choice will present itself. It will be expensive. It will be argued for persuasively by intelligent people with good intentions and a real business case. The answer was written down so that whoever faces it does not have to decide alone.

The corpus is a living constitution, not a museum. Products, markets, technologies, business models, structure, pricing, sectors, countries and leadership should all change, repeatedly — and a future leadership that preserves any of it out of reverence has misunderstood the document. Only the entrenched provisions hold.
